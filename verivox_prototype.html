The challenge

Familiar voices are no longer enough.

Generative speech systems can clone a voice from only a short sample. Attackers use convincing impersonations of executives, public officials, or trusted people to pressure staff into transferring funds, sharing confidential information, or bypassing verification. Caller ID, manual callbacks, and voice familiarity can all be undermined, especially when a convincing story is paired with leaked personal information. Calls may arrive over mobile networks, VoIP, or enterprise collaboration tools. Most workflows still lack an automated, privacy-preserving risk assessment while the conversation is happening.

01
Cheap to imitateA few seconds of recorded speech can seed high-fidelity cloning.
02
High-pressure asksUrgency and sensitive transaction context raise the cost of a missed attack.
03
Weak signals of trustCaller ID and human familiarity are not reliable proof of identity.
04
Missing live defenseTeams need timely risk scoring, safe alerts, and a verified next step.
01 / Idea

VeriVox = “verify” + “voice”

Move beyond a yes/no real-or-fake label: estimate impersonation risk, explain the contributing signals, and turn detection into prevention.

A call-time integrity and response layer

Analyze live or near-live audio in short streaming windows. Fuse authenticity, enrolled-speaker consistency, speech behavior, and transaction context into a dynamic 0–100 risk score. When risk crosses a configurable policy threshold, guide the user to MFA, a known-number callback, supervisor escalation, or a transaction hold. Minimize raw-audio retention and prefer on-device or edge inference when practical.

01

Deepfake detection

Spectral, phase, and spectro-temporal cues indicate possible synthetic or manipulated speech.

02

Speaker verification

Compare against an authorized historical reference when one exists; caller ID alone is not identity proof.

03

Prosody analysis

Track rhythm, pitch contours, pauses, breath, and micro-variation across multiple windows.

04

Context analysis

Enrich with contact, call-origin, transaction intent, and historical fraud indicators.

Risk assessment, not a binary verdictConfidence and reasons are surfaced as a graduated score, with uncertainty visible.

04 / Quick start

What you need. How to use it.

The demo runs in this webpage. No account, installation, or upload to a server is required.

Requirements

Prepare the audio and browser.

For a paired checkOne known-speaker reference recording and one incoming-call recording.
File type and lengthUse clear speech (not tones or music) in WAV, MP3, M4A, or OGG. Each clip should be at least 0.5 seconds. One-pass checks use the first 2 seconds; streaming checks use overlapping 2-second windows, up to 30 seconds.
For live microphoneProvide a reference recording and allow microphone access when the browser asks.
BrowserUse a modern browser with JavaScript enabled. Audio is processed locally in the page.
Steps

Run a call check.

01 · Add a referenceChoose an authorized recording of the speaker you want to compare.
02 · Add the callChoose the incoming-call clip, then select the matching request context.
03 · Start analysisChoose “Analyze pair” for one pass or “Stream call demo” to see scores update by audio window.
04 · Review the resultFor microphone mode, use “Use live mic” after adding a reference. Press “Stop” to end monitoring.
Important: the audio-authenticity cue is an experimental trained baseline; speaker match, prosody, and context remain heuristics. The labels are screening estimates, not proof that a voice is real or fake. Independently verify sensitive requests.

06 / Feasibility and viability

Modular, testable, and built around known risks.

The proposed system is divided into components that can be built and tested independently, then connected after latency, privacy, and reliability have been checked.

ChallengeRisk to the systemMitigation in the proposal
Real-time latencyMultiple models compete with call-time response needs.Optimize inference with ONNX/TensorRT; use VAD and compact models; measure end-to-end latency per window.
Indian language and accent diversityPerformance can shift across languages, dialects, devices, and speaking styles.Transfer learning, data augmentation, representative language/accent evaluation, and disclosed unsupported conditions.
False positives / false negativesA real caller may be challenged, or a spoof may pass.Calibrate on held-out speakers and attack types; track FAR/FRR; retain human escalation and a second verification path.
Biometric privacyVoice samples are sensitive personal data.Edge inference where feasible, minimal retention, feature-only or anonymized logs, encryption, and DPDP-aware controls.
Legacy integrationBanking and telecom workflows use varied platforms and approval systems.Modular REST/gRPC APIs and SDKs; connect through approved service adapters and configurable policy workflows.
Model driftNew synthesis systems and codecs can reduce detection reliability.Review incidents and confirmed outcomes; run controlled retraining and regression tests before model release.
07 / Impact and benefits

Protect trust where a voice can move money.

A reusable verification layer can support people and institutions that still depend on spoken instructions for high-impact decisions.

Bank customers and call-center usersEarlier warnings against voice scams during sensitive requests.
Elderly and vulnerable peopleAdditional protection against emotionally manipulative impersonation.
Banks, telecom, and government helplinesStronger assurance and earlier incident response for voice channels.
EnterprisesLower exposure to fraud-related financial and reputational damage.
Social

Reduce human harm

Help prevent emotional and financial trauma, and build public trust in voice-based services.

Economic

Reduce avoidable loss

Lower fraud losses, disputes, and chargeback costs; support safer voice-led transactions.

Security & compliance

Strengthen resilience

Support incident containment, accountable verification, and a tamper-evident audit design.

Scalability

Designed to extend

Target banking, telecom, and government deployment, with multilingual validation for India's diversity.

Expected outcome

Reduce the likelihood and impact of voice-cloning fraud; surface AI-driven social engineering earlier; improve trust in voice channels; and provide a reusable security layer for telecom, financial, enterprise, and government environments that supports national cyber resilience. Multilingual and regional-accent coverage must be demonstrated through evaluation, not assumed.
